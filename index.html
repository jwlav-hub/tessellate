The Problem

The quantitative core of betting is rented — and underbuilt.

Sportsbooks have invested in brands, apps and customer acquisition. The engine that decides what every bet is worth has been left to a handful of turnkey suppliers.

01

Operators rent their trading brain

Pricing and risk IP belongs to a small group of turnkey suppliers — not to the operator running the book and carrying the liability.

02

The richest markets are priced crudely

Live betting, same-game parlays and props demand the hardest math. They are exactly where books leak the most margin.

03

Informed money is banned, not priced

Books limit sharp customers because their risk systems can't absorb them. In any other market, that is a market-maker's failure.

04

No one runs the book as a portfolio

Exposure is managed event by event, while margin quietly leaks to prediction markets and offshore books.

10–40% of sportsbook GGR

Even the largest operators leave an estimated 10–40% of sportsbook gross gaming revenue on the table through crude pricing, blunt limits and event-by-event risk management.

The Opportunity

Margin is leaking where the math is hardest.

Wagering activity is shifting toward products that price informed flow rather than ban it. Operators who can quote sharper, take more and manage risk across the whole book win that activity back — without giving up their brand or their customers.

$10B+

The Trading Book

Every wager is a position.

Tessellate turns the sportsbook backend into a managed portfolio with a defined risk appetite. Each accepted wager is booked to a portfolio ledger and assessed against the whole book — not just the event in front of it.

Every position is tagged by
  • Event
  • Outcome
  • Bet type
  • Time horizon
  • Customer segment
  • Correlated risk factor
Continuous fair value
Fair value and executable prices update with every data point and every order.
Whole-book decisions
Concentration, same-game correlation, likely payout and stress loss are weighed before a wager is accepted, repriced or declined.
Margin vs. mark-to-market
Traders see the edge being earned apart from the day-to-day swings in the book's value.
